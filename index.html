Wild cats are among the most fascinating and powerful mammals on Earth. Throughout history, they have captured human interest through their strength, agility, and mysterious behavior. From African savannas to tropical forests and mountainous regions, wild cats play a vital role in maintaining natural balance.

How many species of wild cats are there?

There are 41 living species of wild cats in the family Felidae, a group that includes all naturally occurring cat species except the domestic cat (Felis catus). These wild cats range from the massive tiger and lion to much smaller species like the rusty-spotted cat, one of the smallest wild cats in the world. They inhabit a wide variety of ecosystems, including forests, grasslands, deserts, mountains, and wetlands across nearly every continent.

The Lineages

While the total number of recognised species of wild cats varies, the eight lineages that make up the Felidae family are widely accepted. As of November 2017, the Cat Specialist Group of the International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) recognises 41 species in the family Felidae (including the domestic cat). The family Felidae is made up of two subfamilies: Pantherinae, which includes the 7 big cats, and Felinae, which includes the 33 small cats.
